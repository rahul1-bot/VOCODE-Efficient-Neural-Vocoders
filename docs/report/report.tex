\title{VOCODE: A Systematic Evaluation of Quality, Efficiency, and
Deployment Trade-offs in Neural Vocoders}

\author{\name Rahul Sawhney \email rahul.sawhney@fau.de \\
  \addr Department Artificial Intelligence in Biomedical Engineering\\
  Friedrich-Alexander-Universit\"at Erlangen-N\"urnberg\\
  N\"urnberger Stra\ss e 74, 91052 Erlangen, Germany}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}%
Neural vocoders differ sharply in quality, speed, and footprint, yet
published results resist comparison: corpus, training budget,
implementation, evaluator, and hardware vary across papers.  VOCODE
benchmarks twelve vocoders spanning autoregressive, GAN-based,
iSTFT-based, flow-matching, and attention-augmented designs, trained from
scratch on LJSpeech with architecture-native objectives and identical
data splits.  Evaluation is unified: ten quality metrics (PESQ, STOI,
UTMOS, spectral and pitch distances) plus real-time factor, latency,
parameters, and size.  Deployment interventions yield 69 transformed groups paired with 22
same-hardware baselines (273 executions), each judged under an explicit
decision rule: 19 beneficial, 32 harmful, 18 inconclusive.  No vocoder is Pareto-optimal across quality, latency, and
footprint: the speed-quality frontier is Vocos, RFWave, and RNDVoC.  Gains
were architecture-dependent: FP16 halved storage at preserved PESQ in
eight of ten groups, quality-preserving speedups reached $2.07\times$
(ONNX) and $1.40\times$ (compilation), and RFWave at eight ODE steps
gained
$1.20\times$ within 0.05 PESQ.  Masked pruning was harmful in 18 of 19;
static INT8 compressed models near-fourfold at material quality loss.  At
moderate quality, parameter, and storage budgets, selection chose
FP16-cast Vocos on GPU and dynamic-INT8 Vocos on CPU.  With an adaptive
evaluation set, unreplicated training, and no listening test, VOCODE
supports conditional, auditable comparisons, not a causal architecture
ranking.
\end{abstract}
